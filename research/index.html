Signed Once

The asymmetry in semiconductor supply contracts

Every chip company signs a version of the same few deals — with a foundry to make its parts, a customer to buy them, a licensor for the IP underneath. In almost every one, the risk is allocated to a single side, and it lands on the party that signed once — not the counterparty who has signed its version a hundred times. If you are a chip company, a founder, or a buyer, that party is usually you.

And the exposure does not end when the ink dries. The clause you never priced becomes a latent liability — one that resurfaces at your next funding round, your acquisition, or your IPO, when someone on the other side of the table reads the contract you didn't, and turns it into a lower valuation, a stalled deal, or a failed diligence. This is a field guide to that asymmetry, read from real, SEC-filed agreements — depth over size, every figure and quotation traced to the public filing.

Who this book is for

The founders, operators, and procurement teams who sign these contracts, and their boards — and the investors, acquirers, and lenders who need to read the risk before they back them.
