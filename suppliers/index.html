Sample output · Capital investment signal
The design-in is the procurement decision.
When engineering commits to a part, procurement inherits the supplier behind it — their financial health, their technology priorities, and their ability to supply that part for 10 to 15 years. The roadmap describes what a supplier intends to build. The filing tells you whether the capital is there to build it, and whether this technology is still where they are putting it.
Two documents. One supplier. Very different information.
📋

The roadmap

Nodes, densities, capacity plans, product generations. Presented at every supplier review. Describes what they intend to build. Does not say whether the investment is funded — or whether this product line is still a strategic priority.

📂

The filing

Capital spending relative to what the existing plant costs to maintain. Free cash flow. Management commentary on which segments and technologies they are prioritising. Filed with regulators every quarter. Rarely opened by engineering or procurement.

Capital spending vs. plant depreciation — one ratio that points forward

What each supplier is doing with their plant base — FY2025

Think of this as a maintenance dial. At 1.0× a supplier is spending exactly enough to replace what wears out — their factory stays the same size. Above 1.0× they are adding capacity or upgrading to a newer process. Below 1.0× their factory is gradually shrinking. A supplier spending below replacement for several consecutive years is signalling something about the long-term availability of parts from that plant.

Below 1.0× is the clearer signal. A plant base shrinking faster than it is replaced has no positive interpretation for a buyer with a 15-year programme. Above 1.0× is more nuanced — it can be new capacity, a process upgrade, or the same plant rebuilt on a newer node. The ratio shows direction. The transcript tells you which.

The procurement read
The power semiconductor market is structurally bifurcated. SiC is supply-constrained across all three suppliers — limited buyer leverage, price increases being implemented, lead times extending at onsemi. Legacy IGBT and Si power has genuine negotiation room — particularly at STM where P&D is loss-making and the factory needs volume. All three are implementing price increases but the financial justification varies dramatically. Knowing which story you are negotiating against changes the conversation entirely.
