SUPPLIER ANALYSIS · MEMORY AND ANALOG

What the Roadmap Leaves Out

A roadmap says what a supplier intends to build. The filings say whether it can. This is the second half, for the eleven memory and analog suppliers behind a vehicle programme — capacity, investment and staying power, taken from documents each company published itself.

What is in it

Six findings, all from filed figures.

01

A price cycle is running, and it is visible

Six memory makers moved gross margin up together across four quarters, one reaching 84.6%. All five analog suppliers did not move — which is what shows the cycle is memory-specific rather than an economy.

02

Memory’s implied price rose between ×1.5 and ×4

On unchanged unit costs, that is what the margin move implies about price. Over the same quarters analog moved between ×1.02 and ×1.13.

03

The eleven are in very different positions

Three memory suppliers are at their six-year revenue peak. A fourth sits 30% below its own, in the same quarter. The largest fall from peak anywhere in the set is analog.

04

One number on the page points forward

Capital spending against depreciation. Above 1.0× the plant base is growing; below it, what wears out is not being replaced.

05

Some capacity is funded by the cycle, some by the business

SK hynix is building at 2.17× depreciation on a price that rose ×2.54. Texas Instruments spends more, at 2.37×, on a price that moved ×1.10 — and did it through the downturn. For a fifteen-year part that distinction is the one that matters.

06

The scale of the capital problem

One supplier turned $158bn of revenue into $1.3bn of free cash over six years, while spending $62bn on plant. It explains more supplier behaviour than any roadmap slide.

A snapshot of something that moves

Every figure in it has an expiry date, and the analysis names them.

The position is as at September 2026. Each supplier restates where it stands when it next files, and the eleven do not report on the same calendar, so the set is never entirely current at one moment. The analysis names the specific conditions that would make each reading wrong — so a reader can check them against the next set of filings rather than take the document on trust.

A printed page can say where a supplier stands. Only a set that is maintained can say when that stopped being true.
