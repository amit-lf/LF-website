The asymmetry

Your supplier’s commercial team has one job. Yours has twenty.

A supplier’s commercial team spends all of its time on pricing and contract strategy for their product lines. Your team splits that same attention across tens of suppliers, hundreds of part numbers and overlapping contract cycles.

The supplier knows their margin structure, their capacity position and their inventory levels. They price to the edge of what the market will accept. Semitrical gives your team the same read — from the same public data the supplier uses internally.

What the platform does

Four layers of procurement intelligence.

Four readings on every supplier we cover, each moving when that supplier files its results — four times a year. Today they reach you as a written report per supplier rather than a login: nothing for your IT to approve, and something you can forward to whoever needs it.

01

Financial trends

Revenue, margins, cash flow, inventory and capex across your supplier universe — annual trends with trailing quarterly updates, and commentary grounded in the filings themselves.

02

Leverage score

A mechanical leverage score per supplier across four financial dimensions — revenue pressure, margin compression, fixed-cost burden and cash-flow stress. No subjective weighting.

03

Supply signal

A directional read on supply tightness against excess capacity, computed from each supplier’s revenue efficiency against their installed asset base. It separates a genuine shortage from a supplier’s bluff.

04

News radar

News filtered for procurement impact and tagged by supplier, so a report carries what has happened since the filing it is built on — not only the filing.

Who it is for

Built on automotive. Applies wherever you buy chips.

The method and the supplier corpus were built for automotive Tier-1 procurement, where the programmes run longest and the terms bite hardest. The same reading works elsewhere, with different emphasis.

Automotive

Ten to fifteen year platforms against contracts signed during the shortage. Where our corpus runs deepest and the stakes are highest.

Industrial

The same long programmes, the same mature-node suppliers, and the same take-or-pay and non-cancellable terms.

Consumer and compute

Shorter cycles and higher volumes. Leverage shows up in inventory build and the pricing cycle rather than in platform lock-in.

Comms and infrastructure

Different lead times, same asymmetry. Allocation claims, capacity commitments and supplier concentration all read the same way.

What we will and will not say

Everything traces to a document

Figures come from a supplier’s own filed accounts; quotes are matched word for word against the transcript, and one we cannot match is dropped rather than paraphrased.

We do not forecast

Nothing here predicts a shortage, a price move or an event. It reads the position a supplier is already in, from what they have already published.

We say when we do not know

Where a company does not publish a figure, the answer is that it cannot be seen here — not a guess dressed as a finding.
